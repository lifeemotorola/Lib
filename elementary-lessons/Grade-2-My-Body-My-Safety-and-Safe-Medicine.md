# Grade 2 Lessons — My Body, My Safety and Safe Medicine (Health Science, Elementary)

**Level:** Grade 2 (Elementary, ≈ ages 7–8)
**Strand:** Health · taught inside **General Science**, here planned as its own unit
**Unit:** Grade 2, Period VI (Semester Two), drawing also on Period III (Semester One) ·
**Duration:** 4 weeks · 40-minute periods
**Source:** *Liberia National Curriculum — General Science, Grades 1–6*, Ministry of
Education (`uploads/General Science1-6.pdf`): **Guide pp. 24–25** “EXPLORING DRUGS ·
EXPLORING MY BODY” and **Guide pp. 18–19** “EXPLORING HUMAN BODY”. (“Guide p. N” = the
printed guide page.)

---

## How to teach this unit in Grade 2

- **Grade 2 explains, not only repeats.** After the demonstration, ask *why* and
  *how*: “Why does a bad touch have to be told and not kept?” Write their answers
  on the chart in their own words and read them back.
- **Two columns, all month.** Nearly every activity in this unit is a sorting
  task — male/female, safe touch/must be reported, good drug/bad drug,
  clinic/shop — so keep a running class chart with two labelled columns and add
  to it each day.
- **Charts and correct names, clothes on.** The private-parts work is done on the
  chart and the dressed/undressed picture; no child shows or touches anything.
- **Practise the refusal out loud, standing up.** The guide's peer-pressure lines
  (“*Just try once*” · “*It's harmless*” · “*It will make you see things/feel
  happy*”, Guide p. 24) are rehearsed until the answer is loud, calm and short.
- **Assess with a demonstration, an oral test and a short paper.** Grade 2 writes
  a little faster; keep the paper under ten items.

## Before you begin — Safeguarding

The guide's own note on this unit reads: “*Please pay attention to proprietary
information*” (Guide p. 19). In practice:

1. Name the parts correctly on the **chart**, never on a child.
2. Answer only what is asked, briefly and truly: a female can become pregnant
   because the baby grows in the **womb**; a male cannot; the baby is born
   through the birth canal. No more detail than the guide carries.
3. Never ask a child, in class, whether anyone has touched them. If a child
   comes to you: listen once, do not investigate, write their words, tell the
   head teacher and the carer **the same day** (Liberia: police Women and
   Children Protection Unit / Ministry of Gender, Children and Social
   Protection). Teachers do not question the child twice — that is the job of
   the people trained for it.
4. Tell the class the secret rule at the start and every time: *a bad touch is
   never a secret you must keep.*
5. Send the parent notice (Appendix C) home the week before Week 1.

# THE MODEL LESSON — “This is my body, I respect it”

**Where it sits:** Week 2, Period 1 — the body-safety lesson the rest of the unit
depends on. Guide pp. 18–19.
**Class:** Grade 2, 40 minutes.

**Objectives.** By the end of the period each pupil can —
1. point to and name on the chart the parts of the body a vest and pants cover, and state the rule for them;
2. tell a good touch from a bad touch in four given cases, with a reason;
3. perform the five-step safety rule out loud and in role play (say no — go away — tell — keep telling — no bad secrets).

**Materials.** Body chart; the dressed/undressed boy-and-girl picture; the two
hoops with touch cards; paper for the class rule chart; a soft ball for the turn-taking.

**Starter (before the circle).** Children stand in the health circle. Say:
“Today we talk about your body. In this class nobody is laughed at, nobody is
asked to show anything, and **a bad touch is never a secret**.”

### Stage 1 — Introduction (5 minutes)

1. Game: “*This is my —, I use it to —*” with hands, eyes, ears, legs (Guide p. 12
   rhyme, carried into Grade 2).
2. Ask: which parts of the body does a **vest and pants** cover? Take answers
   without naming children; point on the **chart**, not on anyone.
3. Write the topic: **My body belongs to me — I respect it.**

### Stage 2 — Main activities (25 minutes)

1. **Name the parts on the chart (5 min).** Boys have a penis and testicles; girls
   have a vulva; both have chests, and a girl's breasts grow later; the parts
   under underwear are **private (sensitive) parts**. Say the words together,
   plainly, twice — the strangeness goes when a word is used normally.
2. **Difference and equality (4 min).** Two columns on the chart: *different* /
   *the same*. Guide's facts go in the first column: a female can become pregnant
   because a baby grows in the **womb**; a male cannot; the baby is born through
   the birth canal. Then the line the guide gives: “we are both humans, made
   differently but equal, each one deserves respect” (Guide p. 18).
3. **Good touch / bad touch sort (6 min).** Cards are put into the two hoops while
   the class says why: handshake; high-five you agreed to; a nurse's check while
   mother holds you | a touch on the breasts, buttocks, vulva or penis; being made
   to touch somebody; being asked to keep it a secret; being shown naked pictures.
   Rule said together: *a touch of a private part, a touch that frightens you, and
   any touch that must be a secret — all three are reported.*
4. **The five steps, performed (6 min).** In pairs, with the ball to pass the turn:
   (1) **Say NO** — loud, standing up; (2) **Go away** to where people are;
   (3) **Tell** a trusted adult the same day; (4) **Keep telling** until somebody
   acts; (5) **No bad secrets** — and the shame is the doer's, never yours.
   Every child performs step 1 at least once. Teacher corrects volume and posture,
   not the words.
5. **Who can help (4 min).** Name the trusted adults of this school and this
   home: mother/father or carer, the class teacher, the head teacher, the school
   health worker. Two children point them on the class list; the teacher writes
   them on the wall chart and the class reads them.

### Stage 3 — Summary (5 minutes)

- The slogan, three times in chorus, hands on hearts (Guide p. 19):
  “***This is my body, I respect it!***”
- Round the circle, one sentence each: “If somebody touches me wrongly, I …”
- Each pupil ticks their name on the **body-safety chart**; tell the class that
  this chart stays on the wall all term.

### Stage 4 — Evaluation (5 minutes)

| Ask | Expected answer |
|---|---|
| Which parts of the body are private? | the parts a vest and pants cover — breasts, buttocks, vulva or penis |
| Who may touch them? | nobody; a nurse or health worker may check them only with a parent there, to keep you well |
| Is a handshake a good touch or a bad touch? Why? | good — it is wanted, safe and in the open |
| Somebody says “this is our secret”. What do you do? | no bad secret is kept — get away and tell a trusted adult the same day |
| Can a male body get pregnant? Answer with the reason. | no — only a female has a womb where a baby grows |
| **Paper (teacher reads):** draw ✔ or ✘: (a) a child shows his private parts to a friend (b) a girl tells her teacher when an uncle touches her (c) a boy and a girl sit at the same table | ✘ ✔ ✔ |

**Model answers for the teacher** are the right-hand column above: a wrong answer
is re-taught with the chart or the demonstration — in a health lesson a wrong
answer is a habit not yet formed, not a mark to lose.

- Write or draw your two trusted adults in the science journal; the teacher

### Common mistakes, and how to correct them

| You will see | Say / do |
|---|---|
| giggling at the correct names | say the words again, calm and ordinary; the joke dies when the word is normal |
| a child beginning to tell a personal story aloud | thank them quietly, sit them in, and speak to them at the break — the class is not the place |
| “but he said it's a game” | the rule does not ask what it was called: a private-part touch or a kept secret is reported |
| a child says only boys are hurt | wrong — say it to the class: boys can be touched wrongly too, and the five steps are the same for everyone |
| refusal practised as a whisper | stand up, feet wide, look, then say it; do it twice |

### Differentiation

- **Support:** let a pupil point instead of speak; pair with a strong model for
  the refusal; give the five steps as five pictures to put in order.
- **Extend:** pupils write the class rule chart, become the pair-checkers, act
  the “tell the teacher” scene with a new case invented by them, and teach the
  five steps to a Grade 1 class (a real peer-teaching moment the guide's
  discussion activities invite).
- **Separate groups:** where the school's custom asks for it, boys and girls
  split for the 5-minute body-parts part only, each with an adult of the same
  sex; everything else stays with the whole class.

👀 **Look for by Friday (most of the class):** names the private parts on the chart
without giggling; sorts all touch cards correctly and says the reason; performs a
loud, standing “No”; can name two adults in this school they would tell.

## Assessment for the unit

**The guide's strategies, used:** labelled body diagram · oral quiz and written
test · role-play assessment of the refusal (rubric below) · the two-hoop sorting
· key-term spelling · science journal (the heartbeat numbers) · discussion-circle
contribution · group oral report on “what drugs do to a family” · paper true/false
with the false ones corrected.

**Period test blueprint (Grade 2, 20 marks)**

| Section | Items | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| A — Key terms: drug, dose, expiry date, addiction, private parts, trusted adult | 4 × 1 | 4 |
| B — Match the organ to its work (6) | 6 | 6 |
| C — True or false, correct the false (5) | 5 | 5 |
| D — Write the five steps of the safety rule (or put the five pictures in order) | 1 | 5 |

**Rubric for the refusal role play**

| Look | 2 — yes | 1 — partly | 0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Says no clearly, standing, looking at the offerer | loud and steady | says it quietly or sits | does not say it |
| Gives a reason | “it harms my body / my family” | reason is vague | none |
| Leaves and tells | walks to people and names an adult | says it, does not show it | stays |

## Appendix B — Slogans, chants and lines to rehearse

1. **The slogan** (Guide p. 19), three times in chorus: “*This is my body, I
   respect it.*”
2. **The five-step rule**, chanted with fingers up: “*Say NO — Go away — Tell —
   Keep telling — No bad secrets!*”
3. **The medicine song** (to the washing tune): “*Only a grown-up gives me
   medicine, gives me medicine, gives me medicine; only my own and not expired,
   kept up high and locked away.*”
4. **The pressure answers** (Guide p. 24) — say back, standing:
   “*Just try once.*” → “**No. My body belongs to me.**”
   “*It's harmless.*” → “**It harms my lungs, my tummy and my school.**”
   “*It will make you feel happy.*” → “**I am happy already; I do not need that.**”

## Appendix C — Parent notice (copy or read at the PTA)

> Grade 2 is finishing the body-safety part of science: the parts of the body and
> what they do; that the parts underwear covers are private; that a **good**
> touch is wanted and safe and a **bad** touch is always told — with the five
> steps *say no, go away, tell, keep telling, no bad secrets*. We also teach
> medicine safety: only a grown-up or a health worker gives medicine, only the
> medicine meant for that child, at the right dose, checked against the expiry
> date, and kept up high and locked. Nothing in these lessons asks a child to
> show or touch any part of their body. If you would prefer your child not to
> take part in a particular talk, please tell the class teacher and we will give
> the work another way — but the safety rule is the one part we ask every child
> to learn.
