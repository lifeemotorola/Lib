# Grade 5 Lessons — Nutrition, Germs and the Health Services Near Us (Health Science, Elementary)

**Level:** Grade 5 (Elementary, ≈ ages 10–11)
**Strand:** Health · taught inside **General Science**, here planned as its own unit
**Unit:** Grade 5, Period III (Semester One) · **Duration:** 4 weeks · 40-minute periods
**Source:** *Liberia National Curriculum — General Science, Grades 1–6*, Ministry of
Education (`uploads/General Science1-6.pdf`), **Guide pp. 54–55**: the Grade 5 “HEALTH
LESSONS” unit (food and nutrition, how germs enter, HIV/AIDS, how a baby is born,
contraception, risky situations, the health services of the community). (“Guide p. N” =
the printed guide page.)

---

## How to teach this unit in Grade 5

- **Data first, opinion after.** Weigh, measure, count and sort before any
  discussion: the plate, the price, the sugar spoon, the pulse rate, the distance
  to the clinic. Grade 5 argues from numbers, and the guide's science-journal
  assessment depends on it.
- **Body-safe and honest.** The contraceptive table is read as written and
  discussed as written. No jokes about anybody's body.
- **The nearest services are the syllabus.** A lesson is not complete until the
  pupils can name the place they would walk to, the service it gives and the day
  it is open.
- **Assess with a chart, a plan and a letter, not only a paper** — the guide
  finishes this unit with a **ten-year plan** and a written piece; both belong in
  the folio.

## Before you begin — Safeguarding

1. **The chart does the work, never the pupil.** No demonstrations on bodies.
2. **Never ask about anyone's own sexual life or HIV status.** The “risky
   situations” work uses invented case cards only.
3. **Separate small groups on request**, with the health worker.
4. **No shaming.** Abstinence and waiting are taught as a choice the pupil has a
   right to make; contraception is taught as knowledge for life and for the health
   services that exist near them. Nobody is named, laughed at, or left out.
5. **A disclosure is listened to once, recorded in the child's own words, and
   reported the same day** to the head teacher and the carer; in Liberia to the
   police **Women and Children Protection Unit** and the **Ministry of Gender,
   Children and Social Protection**. Recent assault goes to a health facility at
   once (72 hours matters).

# THE MODEL LESSON — “Food, germs and the health services near us”

**Where it sits:** Week 1, Period 1 — the lesson that runs the whole unit's three
threads at once. Guide pp. 54–55.
**Class:** Grade 5, 40 minutes.

**Objectives.** By the end of the period each pupil can —
1. classify **six** given foods into energy / body-building / protective and defend a mixed answer;
2. name **two** roads germs travel and **one** thing the body does to stop them;
3. place **four** community health services in order of nearness (home → community → facility);
4. build a one-day plate for a named child from local foods and say what is missing.

**Materials.** Food cards or real samples (cassava, rice, plantain, fish, dried
meat, beans, groundnut, palm nut, egg, milk, cabbage, bitter leaf, pawpaw, orange) ·
a plate per pair · the community cards (compound latrine · handwashing basin ·
boiling fire · health post · clinic · hospital · pharmacy · TBAs and community
health worker · traditional healer · the market · the pump) · chart paper · the
body-defence chart.

### Stage 1 — Introduction (5 minutes)

1. Write the three headings on the board: **ENERGY · BODY-BUILDING · PROTECTIVE**
   and, under them, one question: *what does each one do for the body?*
2. Hands up round: who ate rice or cassava this morning? Who ate fish or beans?
   Who ate a fruit or a leaf? “Today we find out why all three belong on the plate.”
3. Set the task: each pair will build **yesterday's dinner** and tell what was
   missing, and finish the sentence: *germs travel by …*

### Stage 2 — Main activities (25 minutes)

1. **Teach the three groups (5 min).** Use only Liberian examples the guide names:
   energy — cassava, rice, plantain, yam, breadfruit, sweet potato, coco yam,
   dumboy, sugar; body-building — fish, fresh and dried meat, bush meat, beans,
   groundnut, palm nut, milk and milk products, eggs; protective — cabbage,
   lettuce, pepper, tomato, carrot, onion, waterleaf, bitter ball, bitter leaf,
   pawpaw, banana, orange, lime, jackfruit, mango, African plum. “**And when one
   food is left out for a long time?**” — the body becomes thin or swollen, the
   eyes and skin and bones fail, learning falls; that is **malnutrition**.
2. **Build a plate, in pairs (5 min).** Choose 8–10 cards; put them on a plate
   drawn on chart paper: at least one from every group, with the **cost** written
   from the class market list. Check with the partner: *can a child grow on this?*
3. **The germ's roads (6 min).** Five roads the guide names — **air, food, water,
   touch, insects and animals** — are put on the board. Then the defence: skin keeps
   germs out; mucus and coughing and sneezing throw them back; white blood cells
   eat them; and a **vaccine** trains the body to know a germ first. Two pupils come
   to the front, take a cup of cotton-wool “germs”, spread a pinch on their hands and
   shake hands with three pupils, who touch three more — **before** washing. Count:
   how many carry them now? “That is an outbreak — in twenty seconds, in our class.”
   Then wash, and repeat with the soap and clean water: the count stops at one.
   👀 **Look for:** the class can say the five roads and the four defences without
   the chart.
4. **The community map (6 min).** Four cards go on a line on the wall —
   **AT HOME · IN THE COMMUNITY · AT THE HEALTH POST · AT THE CLINIC/HOSPITAL**:
   boil or treat water · wash with soap · clean the compound and the toilet · the
   pump · the community health worker · the health post · the pharmacy · the
   clinic · the hospital (and, where the family chooses it, the traditional birth
   attendant). In pairs, pupils place **twelve** cards — including the **health
   post**, the **clinic**, the **pharmacy** and the **community health worker** —
   along the line from home to hospital, and say **why the card sits there**.
   The class asks one question: “*Where do you go for a fever on Sunday?*”
5. **The honest line (3 min).** Say it together: “**Food builds the body, clean
   water and soap stop the germ, and the health post and the clinic finish the
   job.**”

### Stage 3 — Summary (5 minutes)

- Round the circle: each pupil says one food group with a local example.
- Then one road a germ travels; then one defence of the body.
- Then one health service and one thing it does: “**the health post weighs
  children and gives vaccines**; **the clinic treats malaria**; **the pharmacy
  sells the right medicine**.”
- Write today's **habit** on the class chart: *wash with soap before eating, and
  after the toilet — every day this week.*

### Stage 4 — Evaluation (5 minutes)

| Ask | Expected answer |
|---|---|
| Give one food from each group. | e.g. rice (energy), fish or beans (body-building), bitter leaf or pawpaw (protective) |
| What is malnutrition? Two causes. | the body is harmed by too little food, or by the wrong food, or by food the body cannot use — from not eating enough, or eating one kind only |
| Name two roads germs travel. | air, food, water, touch, insects and animals (any two) |
| What does a vaccine do? | it shows the body the germ first so the body's defence can fight quickly; a vaccinated body does not become so sick |
| Which place gives antenatal care and the baby's vaccines — the pharmacy or the health post? | the health post or clinic (pharmacy sells medicine) |
| **Written:** “The health service nearest our house is … and we go there for …” | a real named service and a real use |

**Model answers for the teacher** are the right-hand column above: a wrong answer
is re-taught with the chart or the demonstration — in a health lesson a wrong
answer is a habit not yet formed, not a mark to lose.

### Assignment and home practice

- Write **yesterday's** three meals in the journal, mark each food's group, and
  write one thing the family could add.
- Ask a grown-up: which service do you go to when a child has fever for two days —
  the health post, the clinic or the pharmacy? What does it cost? Write the answer.
- Measure your own resting pulse for one minute and record it: tomorrow the class
  compares the numbers.

### Common mistakes, and how to correct them

| You will see | Say / do |
|---|---|
| “sugar gives energy, so sugar is a good food” | energy without protection — too much rots teeth and stores as fat |
| fruit called “just water” | the guide puts pawpaw, mango and orange in the protective group — vitamins |
| “only fat people are malnourished” | the swollen belly of kwashiorkor is also malnutrition: too little body-building food |
| “mosquitoes spread HIV” | they do not — the germ is in blood and body fluid, and the mosquito's mouth does not carry blood from one person to the next; mosquitoes do spread **malaria** |
| the pharmacy as the place for a sick baby | the health post or clinic first (weighing, treatment, vaccines); the pharmacy is for medicine |
| “vaccines are for babies only” | they train the body's defence, and some are given at school age |
| giggling at the body cards | say the words again, calmly; the joke dies with the shame |

### Differentiation (the guide's “inclusive and differentiated learning”)

- **Support:** a three-card sort with colour-coded groups; a pupil who cannot yet
  write dictates to a partner, and answers orally at the circle.
- **Extend:** cost the day's food for a family of five; write a second plate for a
  pregnant woman and explain the change; interview the health post's open days and
  report to the class.
- **Children with learning difficulties:** one group per task, with a real object
  to hold, and the sentence frame “*… belongs to … because …*”.

👀 **Look for by Friday (most of the class):** sorts 10 foods correctly; names the
five germ roads and the body's four defences; explains what a vaccine does;
correctly places 10 community cards; says one service's name and what it gives.

Each is a 40-minute period in the same four-stage shape.

| Period | Focus | The heart of the lesson | 👀 Look for |
|---|---|---|---|
| W1·P2 | Malnutrition: too little, too much, the wrong mix | the three cases (a thin child, a swollen belly, a heavy child); what to add from the market and what it costs | explains three forms and names the fix |
| W1·P3 | The plate on a price: eat well for little | costing sheets, real coins, the “palm-oil and leaf” comparison; find the cheapest protective food | justifies one choice by price |
| W1·P4 · W2·P1 | Skin, mucus and white blood cells; vaccination | the model skin; the walking-germs game; the vaccination card some pupils carry | explains how skin and white cells work |

| W2·P4 · W3·P1 | HIV: the facts, the medicine and the care · safe and unsafe fluids | the two-column list (spread / not spread); **testing is the only way to know**, counselling, and treatment that keeps an adult living and working; and **a baby is not infected if the mother takes her medicine and follows the health worker** · the four safe (handshake, cup, classroom, hug) and four unsafe (blood, sex without a condom, sharing a blade or needle, breast milk where the mother is not on treatment), and the one thing **never** decided by looking: no one's HIV status is guessed or named in class | says two ways it is **not** spread, and the testing line |
| W3·P2–3 | Bad substances · the ten-year plan | the guide's own list (alcohol, tobacco, marijuana, kush, tramadol) and the effects on liver, brain and memory; **the plan:** “write a ten-year plan for your life; who will you be at 20; what must you do now” — read in pairs | one effect per substance and a real plan sentence |
| W3·P4 · W4·P1 | The first-aid kit · the menstrual cycle | name five kit items and give two uses each; the cycle chart: day 1 bleeding, around day 14 ovulation, “sex in that week can lead to pregnancy”, period every 28–30 days; fertilization, implantation, the womb and cord, nine months, birth through the vagina, the **Cesarean section** when the baby cannot come that way | explains ovulation → pregnancy in order |
| W4·P1–2 | Contraception and family planning | the **method table** read and sorted three ways (how it works · how strong · where it is found); the two rules: **abstinence is the only 100% choice for a school-age child**, and **the condom is the only method that also cuts STIs** | names 4 methods, 2 side effects, and where each is found |
| W4·P3 · W4·P4 | A risky situation and the safe decision · the health services of our community | case cards (an older boy with a bottle; a girl alone with a neighbour; a friend offering a cigarette; an offer of money for a lift) answered with STOP-THINK-CHOOSE-REVIEW; then the **twelve community cards** and the walk: which service is nearest our house, and what does it do? | chooses and justifies the safe decision; names 4 services |

## Assessment for the unit

**The guide's strategies, used:** oral quiz (each Friday) · the plate and its
reasoning · the community-card placement · the germ-road diagram drawn from
memory · key-term spelling · the **ten-year plan** written piece and the folio
· the **letter home** written to a younger sibling · discussion-circle
contribution · group oral reports on “the services of our community” · paper
test (blueprint below).

**Period test blueprint (Grade 5, 20 marks, 25 minutes)**

| Section | Items | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| A — Key terms: nutrient, malnutrition, immune system, vaccine, HIV, AIDS, ARV, ORS, ovulation, fetus, Cesarean section | 10 × 1 | 10 |
| B — Sort 10 foods into the three groups | 10 | 10 — 1 mark per correct group, half a mark where the food does double duty |
| C — Germ roads (name three) and body defences (name two) | 5 | 5 |
| D — “Write a balanced lunch for a child of six using foods from our market, and say what each food does.” | 1 | 5 |
| E — “A child has had malaria for two days and is breathing fast. What should the family do, and why not the pharmacy alone?” | 1 | 5 |
| F — Community services: list four and one service of each | 1 | 5 |

Total on the paper: 20 marks, **40 minutes**. Where the school marks out of 20,
use sections A–D and count E and F as class work.

**Rubric for the demonstration of the healthy plate** (10 marks — 2 per row):
one food from each group named · its action stated · a local price given ·
the plate compared with the family's real meal · a realistic change proposed ·
language polite about other people's food.
