## How to teach this unit in Grade 1

- **Show, then let them do.** Every habit in this unit is demonstrated by the
  teacher first (washing, brushing, blowing the nose, covering a cough) and then
  performed by every child, one small step at a time. A Grade 1 child who has
  only *heard* about soap has not learned handwashing.
- **The rhyme is the lesson.** The guide opens with “*This is my —, I use it to —*”
  (Guide p. 12). Say it, point, clap it, let the children finish it. Words learned
  in a rhythm stay.
- **Answer with a full sentence, but accept a good one-word answer** and stretch it:
  “Soap! — Yes: soap lifts the germs off, and water carries them away.”
- **Never ask a child to touch or show a private part.** Name them on the chart,
  with clothes on, using the correct words (Guide pp. 12, 18).
- **Repeat on purpose.** Handwashing appears on Monday, again on Wednesday and
  again next week. Habits need the repetition, not the teacher's novelty.
- **Assess by watching**, plus the Friday 👀 checkpoints, a demonstration test and
  a short paper. Grade 1 writes slowly; keep written work under six items.

## Before you begin — Safeguarding

This unit contains the first body-safety and HIV talk of a child's schooling.
Use the chart, not bodies. If a child says something that worries you, listen
once, do not question the child twice, write down what the child said in the
child's own words, and take it the same day to the head teacher and the carer
(in Liberia: the police Women and Children Protection Unit / Ministry of Gender,
Children and Social Protection). Say plainly to the class that a grown-up who
asks you to keep a touching secret is not keeping a promise — and that telling
is brave, never naughty. For HIV, keep to care and kindness: **no child is ever
asked who is sick at home**, and nobody is pointed at.

## The four weeks at a glance

One 40-minute health period a day. If your timetable gives Science three or four
periods a week, run Weeks 1–2 in the same order and fold the last period of each
week into the following week.

| Week | Focus | What most pupils can do by Friday |
|---|---|
| **1** | My body and what it does · handwashing | name 8+ body parts and one work of each; wash with soap through all 7 steps with the class chant |
| **2** | Caring for each part · private parts and my body belongs to me | say how to care for teeth, skin, hair, eyes, ears, nose; name the covered parts and the rule; say no / go away / tell |
| **3** | Good substances and bad substances · never taste what is not yours | sort 8 items into good/bad; give the reason a child must not drink alcohol or smoke; state the medicine rule |
| **4** | HIV: what a small child should know · caring, not laughing · unit round-up | say two ways HIV is not spread; name two ways to care for a sick person; perform the whole handwashing routine alone; revision and the class health chart |

# THE MODEL LESSON — “Wash, wash: my hands, my body, my germs”

**Where it sits:** Week 1, Period 1 (the unit's first lesson — the habit that the
rest of the strand is built on). Guide pp. 12–13.
**Class:** Grade 1, about 40 minutes.

**Objectives.** By the end of the period each pupil can —
1. name four body parts used for keeping clean (hands, face, teeth, hair) and say what we wash them with;
2. perform the seven handwashing steps with the class chant, with the teacher;
3. say one reason we wash with soap (germs travel to food and people and make us sick).

**Materials.** Soap, basin/bowl of clean water, pouring cup, named towels, a
mirror, the washing poster, one wide bowl of water and ground pepper, waste bin.

**Starter check (before the lesson).** As children come in, look at hands; say
quietly to two or three, “We will wash yours in the lesson.” Do not name them
before the class.

### Stage 1 — Introduction (5 minutes)

1. Sit the class in the health circle. Give the two rules: *we answer honestly*
   and *nobody is laughed at.*
2. Song, with actions: “*This is the way we wash our hands, wash our hands, wash
   our hands … when we want to eat.*” (Guide p. 12.) Sing it once, slowly.
3. Ask: “*What do we wash our hands with? Why do we wash?*” Take four or five
   answers. Write today's topic on the board: **Washing my hands keeps germs away.**

### Stage 2 — Main activities (25 minutes)

1. **Show the parts (4 min).** Point on your own body — hands, face, teeth, hair,
   nose; children point to theirs and say the word. “*This is my hand, I use it to
   eat*.” Two children lead the next four.
2. **The germ story (3 min).** “On a dirty hand there are tiny living things we
   cannot see. They are called **germs**. If they go on the food, they come into
   the tummy and we become sick.” Show the hand, then the food, then the tummy.
3. **Teacher demonstration — the seven steps, chanted (5 min).** Wet both hands →
   soap and bubbles → palms → backs → between the fingers → around each thumb and
   the fingertips → rinse all the soap off → **dry well with your own towel.**
   Children do it in the air with you, without water, while you chant.
4. **The Pepper and Soap test (5 min).** Sprinkle pepper on the water in the wide
   bowl — “the pepper is the germs.” Dip a clean finger: pepper sticks. Rub soap
   on the finger, dip again: the pepper runs away. Ask two children what they saw;
   say it together: “*Soap pushes the germs away. Water alone does not.*”
5. **Everyone washes (8 min).** Two lines to the basin; four children at a time,
   the class chants the steps. ⚠️ **No running water?** One cup to wet, soap, one
   cup to rinse; each child uses their own named towel and no child washes in the
   water another child has rinsed in. Teacher checks each child, says one thing
   each did well, and quietly fixes one thing.

### Stage 3 — Summary (5 minutes)

- Recap in sentences: “*I wash my hands with \_\_\_ before \_\_\_.*” (each child
  finishes one).
- Ask: “Which four times do we wash?” — before eating, after the toilet, after
  blowing the nose, when we come in from outside. Count them on the fingers.
- Each child shows clean hands to a partner; the partner gives a thumb-up. Tick
  the class **habit chart** on the wall (a soap bubble per child for today).

### Stage 4 — Evaluation (5 minutes)

Oral, round the circle, then two on paper (teacher reads them aloud):

| Ask | Expected answer |
|---|---|
| Name two body parts we wash every day. | hands, face, teeth, hair, ears, nose (any two) |
| What do we wash hands with? | soap and clean water |
| Why must we use soap, not only water? | water alone leaves the germs; soap pushes the germs away |
| What did the pepper do when the soapy finger came near? | it ran away from the finger |
| Tell me one time we must wash our hands. | before eating / after the toilet / after blowing the nose / coming in from outside |
| **Paper:** tick ✔ the picture that keeps us clean (soap) and ✘ the one that does not (mud). | soap ✔, mud ✘ |

**Model answers for the teacher** are the right-hand column; for a wrong answer,
re-demonstrate the step rather than re-mark it.

### Assignment and home practice

- Wash your hands with soap before supper tonight while your family counts the
  steps on their fingers.
- Tell your family the four times we wash, and bring the answer to class: which
  time is hardest in your house?
- Draw one thing that keeps your body clean; the teacher writes your sentence under it.

### Common mistakes, and how to correct them

| You will see | Say / do |
|---|---|
| rinsing with no rubbing at all | hand the child the soap and stand beside them for the rub |
| washing the palms only | chant louder on “backs” and “between the fingers”, make them show you the back of the hand |
| leaving hands dripping (then wiping on a friend's towel) | dry is a step, not the end; own named towel only, and never share rinse water |
| playing with water and wasting it | pour from the cup themselves — the pouring *is* the discipline |
| “my mum says water is enough” | agree that water helps, then do the pepper test again for the family at home |

### Differentiation (the guide's “inclusive and differentiated learning”)

- **Support:** let a child copy a partner, or point instead of speaking; put the
  strong child on their right for the first two days; work at the basin with the
  teacher yourself for one child per day.
- **Extend:** a quick child leads the chant, becomes the “soap monitor”, dictates
  a full sentence for the wall chart (“I wash my hands before I eat so germs do not
  come into my tummy”), and teaches a Grade 1 younger partner the four times.
- **Children who cannot yet hold a pencil** answer orally and draw; nothing in
  this lesson depends on writing.

👀 **Look for by Friday (most of the class):** washes through all seven steps with
the chant and needs only one reminder; names the four washing times; says “germs”
and what they do; dries with their own towel and bins nothing on the floor.

## Assessment for the unit

**The guide's strategies, used:** oral quiz (each Friday), the demonstration
test, class and home work, the science journal (the pepper drawing), the
key-term spelling, discussion-circle contribution, group tasks, oral reports
and a short paper-based test.

**Period test blueprint (Grade 1, 20 marks, 20 minutes)**

| Section | Items | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| A — Say the meaning of the word (hygiene, germ, soap, private parts) | 4 oral or tick-box | 4 |
| B — Tick the good, cross the bad (8 pictures) | 8 | 8 |
| C — Put the handwashing pictures in order (4 cards) | 4 | 4 |
| D — One sentence: “I wash my hands before …” | 1 | 4 (2 content, 2 neat sentence) |

**Rubric for the handwashing demonstration** (use it on the Friday of Week 4):

| Step | 2 — does it alone | 1 — with a reminder | 0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| wet and soap | bubbles on both hands | needs the soap handed | not done |
| palms, backs, between fingers, thumbs | all four surfaces | two or three | one or none |
| rinse | no soap left (partner checks) | a little left | not rinsed |
| dry with own towel | dry and folded back | damp or shared | not dried |

## Appendix C — Parent notice (copy or read at the PTA)

> This month in General Science, Grade 1 is learning **how to care for the body**:
> washing with soap, brushing, clean hair and nails, blowing the nose into a
> tissue, and the rule that the parts a vest and pants cover are private and are
> never shown or touched — and that a child who is made uncomfortable should say
> no, go away and tell an adult at once. We also talk about why children must
> never taste medicine, cleaning liquid, alcohol or cigarettes. Please practise
> the seven washing steps at home, keep your child's own towel, and keep all
> medicine and cleaning things **up high and locked**. Nothing is taught in this
> unit by asking a child to show or touch any part of their body.
