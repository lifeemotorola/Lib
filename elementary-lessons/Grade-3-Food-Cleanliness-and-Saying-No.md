## How to teach this unit in Grade 3

- **Start from what the child already eats.** Every nutrient in this unit has a
  Liberian name and a market price. The guide asks for a **catalogue of twenty
  local plants** (p. 32) — build it on the wall, plant by plant, all fortnight.
- **Sort, then explain.** Energy / body-building / protective: children put the
  real foods or pictures into three hoops *first*, and say the reason after.
- **Grade 3 argues.** Ask for a reason in a full sentence — “Groundnut goes in the
  body-building group **because** it repairs the muscle.”
- **Keep the honest tone for the sensitive part.** Bad substances and STIs are
  taught as facts and as protection, with the same four-step refusal the children
  learned in Grade 2. Nothing about any child's own family or body is discussed in
  class.
- **Assess with a chart, a catalogue and a journal, not only a paper.**

## Before you begin — Safeguarding

Grade 3's unit is the first time the national guide names **STIs** to eight- and
nine-year-olds. Keep exactly to what the guide lists: what an infection is, the
germs that cause the common ones, **how they get into the body and how they do
not**, and why waiting is the safe choice. Do not describe sexual acts; do not use
any real person in the community as an example; do not ask which pupils have
relatives who are ill. Where the school's custom allows, separate boys and girls
for the menstruation and body-care talk with a same-sex teacher present (the
guide's own inclusive-learning instruction, p. 32). A child who discloses harm is
listened to once, written down in the child's own words, and referred the same day
to the head teacher and the carer (Liberia: police Women and Children Protection
Unit / Ministry of Gender, Children and Social Protection).

# THE MODEL LESSON — “What on our plate keeps us strong”

**Where it sits:** Week 2, Period 1 — the food and nutrient lesson the rest of the
unit leans on. Guide pp. 32–33.
**Class:** Grade 3, 40 minutes.

**Objectives.** By the end of the period each pupil can —
1. name the three nutrient groups the guide lists (energy, body-building, protective) and give two Liberian foods for each;
2. sort ten given foods into the three groups and say the reason for one;
3. build a plate for one meal that carries **at least one food from every group**.

**Materials.** The food samples/pictures; three hoops labelled **E**, **B**, **P**;
chart paper; the catalogue wall; glue or tape; a market price list written by the
teacher on the board (rice ¢, fish ¢, leaves ¢).

### Stage 1 — Introduction (5 minutes)

1. Question the class: “*What did you eat this morning?*” Write six answers on the
   board. Ask: “*Which one of these made you able to run at break?*”
2. Tell the purpose: foods do **different jobs** in the body, and a good day's
   eating takes at least one food from each job — the guide's own rule for the day
   (p. 32, “match local plants with the major nutrient groups”).
3. Write the topic: **Energy · Body-building · Protective.**

### Stage 2 — Main activities (25 minutes)

1. **Teach the three jobs (6 min).** Put a card on the board for each:
   **Energy (E)** — carbohydrate, fat and oil: rice, gari, cassava, plantain,
   breadfruit, palm oil; **Body-building (B)** — protein: groundnut, beans,
   cowpea, fish, eggs, palm-nut meat; **Protective (P)** — vitamins and minerals:
   bitter leaf, pumpkin leaf, fiofio, orange, pawpaw, guava, carrot, crayfish.
   Say each job in a sentence: “Energy food burns to give heat and strength.”
2. **Guided sort at the board (5 min).** Ten food cards; pupils come up and pin
   them into the right column; the class says ✔ or “move it”, and one child gives
   the *because* sentence for each.
3. **Centre work — the hoop sort (5 min).** In four groups, mixed by ability and
   gender (the guide's inclusive instruction), the real foods or pictures are
   sorted into the three hoops. The teacher circulates and asks only one thing:
   “*Which job does this food do?*”
4. **Build a plate (6 min).** Each group builds **one meal** on paper for a family
   of five from the foods of their own market, using the price list; they must
   carry at least one E, one B and one P. They write the cost under the plate.
5. **Plates up, plates tested (3 min).** Two groups show their plate; the class
   counts the letters aloud. Ask the guide's question back to them: “*Which plant
   can supply our bodies with protein?*” — and the harder one: “*Which group was
   hardest to put on the plate, and why?*”

### Stage 3 — Summary (5 minutes)

- Three sentences, said together, one per group.
- The honest point: “**A plate of only cassava is not a balanced plate** — the
  body needs all three jobs every day.”
- Each pupil adds one plant to the twenty-plant catalogue on the wall, with its
  use and its letter.

### Stage 4 — Evaluation (5 minutes)

| Ask | Expected answer |
|---|---|
| Name the nutrient group that gives energy, with two foods. | carbohydrate/energy: rice, cassava, gari, plantain, breadfruit (any two) |
| Which group builds and repairs the body? Give two foods. | body-building (protein): groundnut, beans, cowpea, fish, eggs (any two) |
| What do protective foods do for us? | keep the eyes, skin, gums, bones and nerves working and guard against disease |
| Your brother eats only rice and palm oil. What is missing, and what could the family add cheaply? | body-building and protective; add groundnut or beans/cowpea to the sauce, dry fish, and a green leaf or fruit |
| **Paper:** write E, B or P beside each: plantain … fish … pawpaw … | P? — plantain **E**, fish **B**, pawpaw **P** |

**Model answers for the teacher** are the right-hand column above: a wrong answer
is re-taught with the chart or the demonstration — in a health lesson a wrong
answer is a habit not yet formed, not a mark to lose.

### Assignment and home practice

- With a grown-up, look at the family's next meal; write the foods on the plate
  and mark each E, B or P. Bring the paper.
- Add one plant to the class catalogue from your own compound (food, medicine or
  material — say which).
- Find one plant at home used as **medicine** and ask what it is used for; tell
  the class next week (no child names a sick person at home).

### Common mistakes, and how to correct them

| You will see | Say / do |
|---|---|
| everything called “healthy” | ask *which job does it do?* — the letter must be said |
| palm oil put in “bad” because it is fat | fat and oil are an energy food in the plate; too much is the problem, not the palm oil |
| a group's plate with only E | hand them the groundnut card and ask what the body builds with |
| sugar called energy food “so it is good for us” | agree it gives energy and no protection — that is why too much spoils teeth and body |
| children naming a neighbour's illness | stop it kindly: “we talk about plants, not about people's sicknesses” |

### Differentiation

- **Support:** give three picture cards, not ten; let the child hand cards to a
  partner who pins them; accept the letter only, then add one word.
- **Extend:** pupils write the “because” sentence for the class chart, cost the
  day's food for a family of seven, or add a fourth column (water and hygiene)
  and present it.
- **Children with difficulty seeing or hearing:** real objects to hold for the
  sort; face the class when speaking; pair with a buddy who describes the card.

👀 **Look for by Friday (most of the class):** names the three groups unprompted;
sorts 10 of 12 foods correctly; says one food from each group for lunch; explains
why one food alone is not enough.

## Assessment for the unit

**The guide's strategies, used:** the twenty-plant catalogue (accuracy and
completeness) · quiz and test on the nutrient groups and hygiene · key-term
spelling · the group sort presented orally · oral report on “how STIs get into the
body and how they do not” · science journal (the seedling record with its graph) ·
paper-based true/false with the false corrected · discussion-circle contribution.

**Period test blueprint (Grade 3, 20 marks)**

| Section | Items | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| A — Key terms: nutrient, carbohydrate, protein, vitamin, hygiene, STI | 6 × 1 | 6 |
| B — Sort eight foods into E / B / P | 8 | 8 |
| C — True or false, correct the false (4) — including “mosquitoes can spread an STI” | 4 | 4 |
| D — Two sentences: how STIs enter the body, and how they do **not** | 1 | 2 |

**Rubric for the catalogue and the plate**

| Look | 2 | 1 | 0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plants named with a use | 8+ correct | 4–7 | fewer than 4 |
| Nutrient group given | all correct | some wrong | none |
| The plate | E, B and P present, costed | two groups | one group |
