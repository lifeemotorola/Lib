# Elementary (Grades 1–6) Health Science Lessons — Liberian National Curriculum

Lesson plans for the **health strand of the national Elementary General
Science curriculum**, Grades 1 to 6 — the half of the guide that keeps a child
alive and well: body parts and how to care for them, hygiene and handwashing,
food and nutrients, germs and the body's defence, medicines and drug abuse,
body safety and bad touches, puberty, HIV/STIs, communicable disease, malaria
and healthy living.

They are written from the official curriculum guide kept in this repository:
**`General Science 1-6`** (Republic of Liberia, Ministry of Education —
`uploads/General Science1-6.pdf`, 78 pp.). Every outcome, objective, content
line, activity, material and assessment strategy below is the guide's own; the
page it comes from is cited at the head of each section ("Guide p. N" — the
printed page number inside the guide, which is also the PDF page number).

## What the guide actually asks for (the strand at a glance)

The guide does not have a separate "Health" subject: health content sits inside
General Science, in one or two units per grade, and it spirals — the same idea
comes back harder each year.

These files plan that content as **six units per grade — Health Science Periods
I to VI** (Periods I–III in Semester One, IV–VI in Semester Two), four weeks
each. The period the guide gives the health content to keeps it at that period
number; the other periods teach the same grade's own outcomes, objectives,
contents and activities again, one unit at a time. Nothing here is invented
outside the guide — every unit's subtitle cites the guide page it is taken
from.

| Grade | Periods (guide) | The health ideas the guide puts in it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Part VI — Care of the Body | body parts and their work · hygiene · **handwashing** · private parts · good and bad substances · what a small child should know about HIV |
| 2 | Part III / Part VI | major body parts, male and female, **sensitive parts**, good and bad touch · what a drug is, **addiction**, medicine safety, refusal |
| 3 | Part IV | local plants as **food and medicine**, the nutrient groups, personal hygiene, how bad substances **enter the body**, **STIs** — how they do and do not spread, refusal skills |
| 4 | Part VI (+ Part V) | **puberty** and body changes, hygiene that grows with the body, good/bad touch and **reporting**, pregnancy facts · skin layers and **malaria** prevention |
| 5 | Part III | **nutrition** and the three food groups, the **immune system**, HIV and nutrition, conception, **contraceptives**, abstinence, risky situations, **community health services** |
| 6 | Part IV (+ Part II) | the **body systems**, the reproductive system, sexual identity and respect, **risky behaviour and STI/HIV**, communicable vs non-communicable disease, exercise, rest, diet, first aid |

Notice what the guide *repeats*: handwashing (G1, G3, G6), the private parts and
bad-touch rule (G1, G2, G4), the drug and refusal line (G1, G2, G3), HIV facts
and **no stigma** (G1, G5, G6). That repetition is deliberate — a habit is
taught by coming back to it, not by covering it once.

## How the lessons are built

- **One 40-minute period, four timed stages** — Introduction, Main activities,
  Summary, Evaluation — exactly the shape the sector's lesson-plan form expects
  (the same timings the app prints).
- **Demonstrate → practise → record.** Nothing in a health lesson is finished
  until every pupil has done the thing: washed, brushed, refused, counted the
  pulse, pressed the cloth, labelled the chart.
- **Assessment is oral, demonstrative and written** — the guide's own list:
  quizzes, test, class and homework, assignments, science journal, key-term
  spelling, discussion circle, group tasks, oral reports, paper-based work.
- **Friday checkpoints (👀 Look for)** close each week in the model lesson and
  the scheme, so a teacher can record the class in one line.

## Safeguarding (read before Grade 2, and every time after)

The national guide puts sensitive content in the primary science classroom —
private parts at six, puberty at nine, contraception and HIV at ten to twelve.
It also prints its own warning: *"Please pay attention to proprietary
information"* (Guide pp. 19, 25, 47). Every lesson file repeats the same rules:

1. **Charts, pictures and correct names — never bodies.** Pupils never undress,
   never show and never touch one another; the chart does the work.
2. **Same-sex small groups** for the body-change and menstruation talk where the
   school's custom allows it, each led by a same-sex teacher or the school health
   worker (the guide's own instruction for Grades 4–6).
3. **No child speaks about their own home or their own body in front of the
   class.** Case studies use invented characters only.
4. **Nothing is kept secret.** Say it out loud at the start of any body-safety
   lesson. A disclosure is listened to **once**, written in the child's own
   words, and taken **the same day** to the head teacher and the carer — in
   Liberia, to the police **Women and Children Protection Unit** and the
   **Ministry of Gender, Children and Social Protection**; a recent assault goes
   to a health facility at once (72 hours matters for care and evidence).
   Teachers do not investigate.
5. **Stigma is taught as a health danger**, not a joke. No pupil is named,
   laughed at or left out because of an illness, a family's illness or a body.
6. **Tell the parents first.** Every file carries a short parent notice; send it
   or read it at the PTA meeting the week before the sensitive lessons.

## Using these lessons with the course-pack app

The same content is wired into the generator as the **Health Science** subject:
choose *National Curriculum → Health Science → Grade 1 … Grade 6*.

- **Lesson plan** (daily or weekly, 4 weeks per unit, 40 minutes) — the health
  shape printed on the sector form: the health-circle opening, the
  demonstration, the practice in pairs, the class health chart, home practice,
  the health fair for the last week, and the **safeguarding note on every plan**.
- **Course pack** — pupil workbook with the key-terms glossary, matching,
  cloze, true/false, short answer, MCQ, sorting, label-the-diagram,
  investigation write-up and apply-it questions, plus the period test, the
  semester examination and the teacher's answer key with reasons.
- Tick **two or three units** (Grades 1–6 units are numbered Period I to Period
  VI, e.g. Grade 1 Period III — Washing My Hands) and export to Word or print
  as A4.

Kindergarten's `Staying Healthy and Safe` lessons live in `../kg-lessons/`; the
Elementary files here are the same strand grown up — from "wash your hands
because germs travel" to "wash your hands, and here is the outbreak it stops".

## Notes on the source

- The guide's grade pages are laid out in columns — *Outcomes, Objectives,
  Contents, Activities, Materials/Resources, Competences/Assessment* — and each
  lesson file reproduces that block for its grade before teaching it.
- Where the guide's own spelling varies ("color", "facilitator", "Ulize"), the
  lessons use ordinary Liberian schoolroom English and keep the guide's wording
  wherever the wording matters (the rhymes, the slogans, the activity names).
- The Grade 6 unit cites the guide's own primary textbook line
  (Darsaw Arthur & Fischer-Buder Karin, *New Elementary Science for Liberia,
  Grade 6 Pupil's Book*, Pearson 2014) because the guide names it as a material.
