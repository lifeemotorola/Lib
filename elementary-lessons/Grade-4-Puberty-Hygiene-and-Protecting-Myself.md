# Grade 4 Lessons — My Changing Body: Puberty, Hygiene and Protecting Myself (Health Science, Elementary)

**Level:** Grade 4 (Elementary, ≈ ages 9–10)
**Strand:** Health · taught inside **General Science**, here planned as its own unit
**Units:** Grade 4, Periods I–VI (three in Semester One, three in Semester Two) ·
**Duration:** 4 weeks per unit · 40-minute periods
**Source:** *Liberia National Curriculum — General Science, Grades 1–6*, Ministry of
Education (`uploads/General Science1-6.pdf`): **Guide pp. 48–49** “HEALTH AND PERSONAL
HYGIENE” and **Guide pp. 46–47** “EXPLORING SKIN, SOIL AND SOIL CONSERVATION”. The skin
and malaria work belongs to the other unit; it is used here because the Grade 4 health
lessons keep referring to skin, sweat and “itchy” complaints. (“Guide p. N” = the
printed guide page.)

## How to teach this unit in Grade 4

- **Two voices, one lesson.** Read the changes **aloud**, then let pupils write
  **silently** for two minutes in their journals. The pause is not lost time: many
  nine- and ten-year-olds need it, and writing is how the guide's journal
  assessment gets made.
- **Say everything you say twice** — once for the class, once for the pupil at the
  back who heard it but is pretending not to have.
- **Boys with the men, girls with the women — the guide's own instruction.** The
  menstruation talk and the “wet dreams and chests” talk run **separately**, each
  led by a same-sex teacher or the school health worker (pp. 48–49); the hygiene,
  safety, malaria and reporting lessons stay with the whole class.
- **Charts and correct names, clothes on.** The chart does the work; no child's
  body is ever shown or discussed in front of the class.
- **Assess with sort cards, the two-column chart, the demonstration, the first-aid
  test — and always the reason**, not the blush.

## Before you begin — Safeguarding (read this with the head teacher)

1. **The parents are told before the pupils are.** Send Appendix C the week before
   Week 1 and offer the family the right to teach that lesson at home, with the
   class doing the hygiene and safety work instead.
2. **No child speaks about their own body or their own home in front of the
   class.** Case studies use invented characters; never name a real pupil or a real
   family.
3. **Separate groups where custom asks for it:** girls with the female teacher or
   school health nurse, boys with the male teacher, for the body-change part only.
   The rest of the unit is common work.
4. **Nothing is kept secret.** Say it at the start of every body-safety lesson: *a
   grown-up who touches you wrongly and asks you to hide it is not keeping a
   promise.*
5. **If a child discloses:** listen **once**, do not question further, write the
   child's own words with date and time, tell the head teacher and the carer the
   same day, and in Liberia report to the police **Women and Children Protection
   Unit** and the **Ministry of Gender, Children and Social Protection**. A recent
   assault goes to a health facility **at once** — 72 hours matter for medical care
   and for evidence. Teachers never investigate, never promise secrecy and never
   send the child back to sit in the same house unaided.

## The four weeks at a glance

One 40-minute health period a day, in this order. Where the school prefers
separate boys'/girls' sessions for the body-change parts, run those as one period
of the week and keep the rest with the whole class.

| Week | Focus | What most pupils can do by Friday |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Puberty defined; the physical changes of boys and girls | state what puberty is; place 8 change-cards in the right column, boys or girls |
| **2** | Looking after the changing body: skin, sweat, clothes, pads, face; mood and privacy | demonstrate the care steps on the chart; state the two daily washing habits; sort mood changes from body changes |
| **3** | My body is mine: good and bad touch, the six-step reporting, the class rules chart | perform the four steps; recite “it's not your fault”; sort safe/unsafe situations |
| **4** | Skin, malaria and the first-aid kit (the guide's pp. 46–47 health work), then the unit test | state 4 malaria controls; demonstrate care of a cut and a burn; pass the unit test |

**The sensitive part comes first on purpose:** a nine-year-old whose body has
already started changing needs the vocabulary in week one, not week four.

# THE MODEL LESSON — “Growing up: the changes, and how I care for my changing body”

**Where it sits:** Week 1, Period 2 (Period 1 defines puberty and sets the rules).
**Class:** Grade 4, 40 minutes. Guide pp. 48–49.

**Objectives.** By the end of the period each pupil can —
1. name at least four body changes that happen to **girls** and four that happen to **boys** at puberty;
2. state **two** hygiene habits that the changing body needs, and **why** each one matters;
3. say one mental or social change (mood swing, shyness, wanting privacy) and one healthy way to handle it.

**Materials.** Boys'/girls' change boxes and cards · the undressed and dressed
charts · the clean cloth pad, laundry soap, washcloth, small towel, mild soap, nail
scissors, deodorant, aftershave lotion, toothbrush and paste, mirror · chart paper
and markers for each group · pupils' journals.

**Before you start (the day before):** pupils have heard the parent note; the
day's seating and the two same-sex groups are arranged with the head teacher.

### Stage 1 — Introduction (5 minutes)

1. Health circle. Re-read the three rules aloud, with the class: *charts, not
   bodies* · *right words, calm voices* · *nobody's own story in front of the class*.
2. Quick review: “What is puberty?” — the time the body grows into an adult's body.
   “When does it start?” — usually around 9 to 13, and not all children at once.
   “Whose body changes faster?” — nobody's: it is different for each child.
3. Ask each pupil to write one word in the journal for how they feel about growing
   up. Two minutes in silence. No names read aloud; the teacher reads two or three
   anonymous ones: “*excited*”, “*shy*” — “both words are right.”

### Stage 2 — Main activities (25 minutes)

1. **Whole class, charts only (6 min).** Put the two undressed charts side by side.
   Name the parts that change at puberty with the correct words — breasts, hips,
   pubic hair, underarm hair, penis, testicles, face hair, voice box. Say:
   *every body of every child in this room will go through this, and no child's
   body is a joke.*
2. **Same-sex small groups, 8 min each, run in parallel** (girls with the female
   teacher or the school health nurse; boys with the male teacher).
   - **Girls:** the four changes (wider hips, breasts, underarm and pubic hair,
     periods) and the five care steps from the guide — *use clean, soft pads, keep
     them dry, change and wash pads, wash the genital area with clean water and mild
     soap, dry from front to back, wash the cloth with laundry soap and dry it in
     the sun, and do not put things inside the vagina*. Ask for a woman they can go
     to: mother, aunt, the nurse, the female teacher. Practice: fold a clean pad,
     and say the “who do I ask” sentence aloud.
   - **Boys:** the seven changes (face, underarm and pubic hair; penis and
     testicles growing; an erection that may come without warning; broad shoulders
     and muscle; the voice changing, deepening, cracking and turning hoarse; more
     sweat and oil; wet dreams at night) and the care steps — *wash every day,
     including under the foreskin, with water only; keep the penis clean; do not
     squeeze the pimples or blackheads; trim the nails; wash hands before eating;
     don't wear too tight pants; use deodorant or powder when the armpits sweat*.
     Say the sentence together: *an erection is not something to be ashamed of —
     it is what a boy's changing body does, and it goes away.*
   👀 **Look for:** each group can state **three** changes and **two** care habits.
3. **Back together — sort and reason (7 min).** Two hoops on the wall: **BOYS'** and
   **GIRLS'**. Groups place the change cards, then must justify each placement —
   *because* the change belongs to that body, or *because* it happens to both.
   Deliberately mix in the shared cards (sweat, body odour, hair under the arms,
   spots, mood swings, wanting privacy, growing taller). Any card that lands in
   the wrong hoop is moved by a pupil, not the teacher.
4. **Mind and feelings (4 min).** Take **two** cards only: “becoming easily angry or
   annoyed” and “becoming sensitive to what others say or do” (the guide's own
   wording, p. 48). Ask for one healthy way to handle each: count to five, walk,
   write in the journal, pray, talk to a trusted adult, sleep on it, laugh about
   it — all are right, none is weak. Then the social card: “feeling shy in front of
   others, wanting more privacy” — privacy is normal, **but a secret about a body
   is not the same as privacy.** That sentence is the bridge to Week 3.

### Stage 3 — Summary (5 minutes)

Each group writes and reads **one** rule for the class chart; it goes up on the
wall for the whole term:

- *We wash our changing bodies every day — body, hair, face, private parts.*
- *We wash and dry our cloth and our pads in the sun, and keep them clean.*
- *We do not squeeze pimples, and we do not put anything inside the vagina.*
- *We wear loose, clean clothes and dry underwear.*
- *We do not laugh at anybody's body — a body that is changing is not funny.*
- *Sweat and smell are normal: wash, and use deodorant or powder if you have it.*
- *If a girl starts her periods, she tells a woman she trusts. If a boy has a wet dream, he washes and tells nobody's business but his own — and neither is a shame.*
- *When the feelings swing, we name them and choose something safe to do with them.*
- *Privacy is right; secrecy about a body is not.*
- *Ask before you touch anybody, and never touch a private part of anybody.*

### Stage 4 — Evaluation (5 minutes)

Oral, round the circle; then two written items.

| Ask | Expected answer |
|---|---|
| Name two changes that happen to girls at puberty. | breasts growing, hips widening, underarm/pubic hair, periods starting (any two) |
| Name two changes that happen to boys. | face and underarm hair, voice deepening or cracking, penis and testicles growing, shoulders broadening, wet dreams (any two) |
| A girl's period has started. What does she do? | tell a woman she trusts; use a clean soft pad and keep it dry; wash the area front to back with clean water |
| Why must a boy wash his body, and not only his hands? | the oil and sweat glands become active — sweat, smell and pimples build up; washing every day keeps the skin and private parts clean |
| A friend's voice cracks and the class laughs. What happens in this class instead? | nothing funny: the voice is changing, we do not laugh, we say “your voice will settle” |
| Write one sentence: “My body is changing, so I will …” | a real daily habit (wash with soap daily / wash and dry the cloth in the sun / trim nails / stop squeezing pimples) |

**Model answers are the right-hand column.** Any answer that starts with a laugh,
a name or a real family story is met with the three rules and redirected: “*which
change is it, and what does the body need?*”

### Assignment and home practice

- Write in the journal: **two** care habits you did at home tonight (washing with
  soap, drying the cloth, clean underwear, trimming nails). No sharing aloud.
- Check your own kit and bring the list of what it needs: soap, clean cloth,
  toothbrush, nail cutter, comb. If something is missing, ask a grown-up for it.
- Family talk (with a grown-up): who is the **woman** this girl and the **man**
  this boy will go to if a change worries them? Name them in the journal.

### Common mistakes, and how to correct them

| You will see | Say / do |
|---|---|
| giggling at every word | name it openly: “We laugh because it is new. Say the word again, slowly — the laugh goes away.” Then continue. |
| pointing at a classmate's body | stop the lesson, re-read rule three, no names; speak to that child after the period |
| a pupil starting to tell a private family story | “That is for you and me at the break — it is not for the class.” Write it down; follow the safeguarding route the same day if it is a disclosure. |
| “periods are dirty” | correct it as science: the womb grows a soft lining ready for a baby; when there is no baby, the lining comes out as blood; it is not dirt, and the blood is normal. |
| a girl hiding a start of periods | private word after class, clean pad from the class kit, and the woman she named; never announced to the class. |
| boys testing the “erection” joke | say it once, matter-of-fact: “the guide tells us the penis may become hard without warning; that is a healthy body doing what bodies do — no comment on anybody's body.” Move on. |
| “deodorant costs money so we can't be clean” | washing with soap and drying well is the habit; deodorant is a bonus, not a requirement. |

### Differentiation (the guide's “inclusive and differentiated learning”)

- **Support:** cards with pictures and one word; a pupil may **point** to the chart
  instead of speaking; the “why” sentence is finished by the teacher and repeated.
- **Extend:** pupils write the class chart in full sentences for the wall, become
  group readers, and answer the *why* question in three sentences (sweat glands,
  bacteria and smell; oil and blocked pores; hormones and voice).
- **Literacy support:** every key word is on a card with a picture —
  *puberty · periods · pads · erection · wet dream · hygiene · deodorant · foreskin
  · private parts · trusted adult*.
- **Shy or anxious pupils** may hand in a written answer instead of speaking, and
  may ask any question on paper into the class box; every box question is answered
  aloud in a general way next period, with no name and no guessing who asked.

👀 **Look for by Friday (most of the class):** says what puberty is in one
sentence; places the change cards for boys, girls and both without giggling;
states three hygiene habits the changing body now needs and why each one matters;
names the change that worries them most and the adult they would ask; and can
recite the six-step safety rule standing up, out loud.
